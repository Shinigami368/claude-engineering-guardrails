# Lead Magnet Format Guide

Use this guide to choose a lead magnet format that matches the user's problem,
buyer stage, and delivery constraints.

## Selection Rules

- Choose the smallest format that solves one meaningful problem well.
- Prefer formats that deliver value quickly after capture.
- Match production effort to the likely business value of the offer.
- Avoid formats that require ongoing maintenance unless the workflow depends on
  up-to-date content.

## Format Fit

| Format | Best For | Use When | Avoid When |
|--------|----------|----------|------------|
| Checklist | Fast tactical wins | The audience needs a quick implementation aid | The topic needs explanation or nuance |
| Template | Repeatable work products | Users want a starting artifact they can adapt | The value depends on extensive customization |
| Swipe file | Messaging or creative inspiration | The audience benefits from concrete examples | The offer would feel derivative without context |
| Guide or ebook | Multi-step education | The user needs broader framing plus actionable steps | The team cannot support a longer asset |
| Calculator or worksheet | Structured decision support | The offer depends on inputs and simple modeling | The logic would be too shallow to justify the format |
| Resource pack | Bundled practical assets | Several small artifacts together solve the same problem | The bundle would become a generic dump |

## Buyer-Stage Fit

- Early awareness: checklists, short guides, templates
- Mid-funnel evaluation: worksheets, comparison guides, implementation packs
- Late-stage enablement: ROI tools, migration templates, rollout kits

## Delivery Notes

- Promise one clear outcome in the title and landing-page copy.
- Keep the thank-you-page next step aligned with the offer's use case.
- If the asset teaches a workflow, pair it with an email follow-up or product
  walkthrough rather than a generic newsletter invitation.
